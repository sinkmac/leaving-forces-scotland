⚠️ Scotland has its own disability payment system

Adult Disability Payment (ADP) has replaced PIP in Scotland. If you have a disability or long-term health condition, apply through mygov.scot/adult-disability-payment — not through the DWP process used in England. Income and savings are not taken into account. You can be working or not working — it makes no difference to eligibility. ADP has two components: daily living and mobility. It is not a veteran-specific payment — it covers any disability or long-term health condition, including those related to service.

What to say — the exact words that work

These phrases invoke specific rights. Write them down before you make the call.

At the council housing office:
'I am a recently discharged veteran and under the Housing (Scotland) Act 2014 I am requesting that my application be assessed with local connection under the veteran provision.'

At your GP surgery:
'I am a veteran. I would like my veteran status flagged on my NHS Scotland record. I would also like to know if this practice is part of the GP Veterans Recognition Scheme.'

To Social Security Scotland about ADP:
'I am applying for Adult Disability Payment. I am a veteran and my condition is service-related. I would like to speak to a veteran-aware adviser.'

To Veterans UK about your pension:
'I would like a pension forecast for my service. My service number is [your number]. I served under [AFPS 75 / AFPS 05 / AFPS 15].'
Call: 0808 1914 218 (free, Monday to Friday)

To a benefits adviser about compensation:
'My Armed Forces Compensation Scheme payment and/or War Pension must be fully disregarded as income in any means-tested benefit calculation. This is a legal requirement. Please confirm you are applying this.'

To any public body not applying the Covenant:
'I am a veteran and I am asking you to apply the Armed Forces Covenant duty to my case. If you are unable to do this, please tell me your complaints procedure.'

If someone says no and you know they are wrong:
'I would like that in writing please.'

Before you leave — do these first

▼

Your access to the Defence Medical Services ends on discharge. Register with an NHS Scotland GP as soon as possible — ideally before you leave. Tell them about your service history.

Request copies of your medical records from the Defence Medical Information and Documentation Centre (DMIDC) before you leave. They're much harder to obtain after discharge.

There are three main schemes:

AFPS 75 — if you joined before April 2005. Deferred pension payable at age 60 if you leave before 16 years service.

AFPS 05 — if you joined between April 2005 and April 2015. Deferred pension payable at age 65 if you leave before the pension point.

AFPS 15 — if you joined after April 2015, or were moved across. Deferred pension payable at state pension age if you leave before the pension point.

If you are leaving before your pension point, your pension is deferred — it is not lost, but it will not arrive when you might expect it. This is the single most common financial planning mistake in the transition year.

Early Departure Payment (EDP): if you have served 18 or more years and are under 55, you may receive an immediate lump sum and monthly payments rather than a deferred pension.

Medical discharge: if you are medically discharged, your entitlement is calculated differently — contact Veterans UK for your specific position.

War Pension vs AFCS: if your condition or injury is from before April 2005, you may be entitled to a War Pension rather than, or as well as, AFCS. These are separate schemes.

Get your personal pension forecast: call Veterans UK on 0808 1914 218 (free, Monday to Friday).

GOV.UK pensions and compensation guidance

You are entitled to Individual Resettlement Training Costs (IRTC) and a resettlement grant. Use your entitlement before leaving — you cannot access it after discharge.

If you live in Service Accommodation, start the process early. Housing Options Scotland Military Matters provides free specialist housing advice for veterans in Scotland. Do not wait until you have left.

Health and injury support

▼

Get a review for long-term service injuries — VISIN

If a military-related injury or condition has not been fully resolved, ask to be referred to the Veterans In-Service Injury Network (VISIN). Hosted by NHS Highland with Scottish Government funding, VISIN provides an independent multidisciplinary clinical review. Ask your GP or health point of contact to refer you.

Women veterans

If you are a woman veteran, the standard transition checklist applies to you — but some things work differently in practice.

Tell your GP you are a veteran. GPs may not ask. Service-related mental health conditions in women are often misread as stress, anxiety, or relationship difficulties. Telling your GP explicitly, and asking for your veteran status to be flagged on your NHS Scotland record, means you are more likely to be routed to the right support.

Veterans First Point works with women veterans. V1P staff are trained in veteran-specific presentations. Self-referral — no GP required.

If you have caring responsibilities that accumulated during the posting cycle — including gaps in your NI record or employment history — these may affect your benefit entitlements. Get advice specific to your situation from Citizens Advice Scotland before claiming.

The Scottish Government has asked Veterans Scotland to improve inclusion of female veterans and recognition of different service experiences. If you feel a service is not recognising your experience, you are entitled to say so.

Housing

▼

You can apply to a Scottish council housing list before you leave. Veterans can be prioritised under the Veterans Homelessness Prevention Pathway — contact your local council housing department and declare your veteran status.

Scotland has stronger homelessness protections than elsewhere in the UK. If you are at risk of homelessness on discharge, your local council has a legal duty to provide temporary accommodation while your case is assessed.

Leaving service accommodation

If you are in service accommodation, you need to start the housing process earlier than you think. Service accommodation ends on or shortly after your discharge date — there is no automatic extension.

Steps to take before you leave:

  • Contact Housing Options Scotland Military Matters as early as possible — free, specialist. housingoptionsscotland.org.uk/military-matters
  • Apply to your local council housing list before you leave — you can apply while still serving.
  • If you are at risk of having nowhere to go on discharge day, tell your welfare officer and contact your local council homelessness team now, not on the day.

If you have nowhere safe on discharge day: Contact your local council and say you are homeless or at risk of homelessness. The council has a legal duty to provide temporary accommodation while your case is assessed. Do not sleep rough before making this contact.

What to say at the housing office

When you contact your local council housing office, use this exact phrase:

'I am a recently discharged veteran and under the Housing (Scotland) Act 2014 I am requesting that my application be assessed with local connection under the veteran provision.'

This phrase matters. Without it you may go into the general queue. With it, you trigger a specific legal entitlement. Write it down before you call.

The Armed Forces Covenant — what it actually means for you

The Armed Forces Covenant is a legal duty, expanded under 2026 legislation. Public bodies must not disadvantage you because of your service.

It covers: housing, healthcare, education, employment, social security, criminal justice, pensions and compensation.

If it is not being applied, say explicitly: 'I am a veteran and I am asking you to apply the Armed Forces Covenant duty to my case.'

Contact the Scottish Veterans Commissioner if needed. scottishveteranscommissioner.org

Benefits and finance

▼

You may be eligible for Universal Credit from the day of discharge. Apply early — there is a five-week wait for first payment. Armed Forces Compensation Scheme payments do not count as income for UC.

Before you claim Universal Credit — what to know first

The five-week wait: UC takes five weeks to pay out from your first claim. You can request an advance payment, but this is a loan repaid from future UC payments — plan for a cash-flow gap in week one.

AFCS and War Pension are fully disregarded: Armed Forces Compensation Scheme payments and War Pension awards must not be counted as income in your UC calculation or any other means-tested benefit. This is a legal requirement. If an adviser counts them as income, challenge it.

Scotland-specific top-up: if you have housing costs, Scotland's Choices element means your UC housing support can be paid direct to your landlord rather than to you — useful during a disruptive transition period.

Work and UC: if you take part-time or temporary work during transition, earnings above your work allowance reduce your UC pound for pound above a taper rate. Get advice before taking short-term work to understand the net effect.

Get advice before you claim: the interaction between your pension, any compensation, and UC is complex enough that a wrong assumption in week one can take months to fix. Citizens Advice Scotland can model your specific situation for free.

Scotland administers several benefits independently: Scottish Child Payment, Best Start Grant, ADP, and others. Check Social Security Scotland for the full list.

Check your NI record at gov.uk. Gaps in NI contributions affect your State Pension. Voluntary NI contributions can fill gaps.

Credit Their Service provides free specialist financial guidance for veterans. Better to get advice before debt becomes a crisis.

Your financial rights — what the system must do

The Scottish Veterans Commissioner's Veterans and Finance report (2025) identified these specific failures. Know your rights:

Compensation disregards: all armed forces compensation, including War Pension awards, must be fully disregarded when calculating income for means-tested benefits including housing benefit and council tax reduction. If a local authority or adviser is not doing this, they are non-compliant. Ask them to check.

Veteran-aware advice: Social Security Scotland and government-funded debt advice services should have veteran-aware staff. If the person you speak to does not understand your situation, ask for someone who does or contact Veterans Gateway for a referral.

Gambling harms: veterans are identified as a heightened-risk group. If gambling has become a concern during transition, GamCare and BeGambleAware offer free, confidential support.

LGBT+ veterans

Fighting with Pride offers specialist support for LGBT+ veterans — welfare, advocacy, and community. fightingwithpride.org.uk

LGBT Financial Recognition Scheme: if you were discharged or asked to resign because of the pre-2000 ban on LGBT+ people serving, you may be entitled to a financial recognition payment. The UK-wide scheme opened in December 2024. Check current status and how to apply at gov.uk/guidance/lgbt-veterans-financial-recognition-scheme.

Scotland-specific: payments from the LGBT Financial Recognition Scheme do not affect your Council Tax Reduction in Scotland. Scottish legislation was amended in 2025 to protect these payments specifically.

The Scottish Government funds Fighting with Pride and has committed to improving support for LGBT+ veterans. If you need a starting point, Fighting with Pride is it.

Employment and training

▼

Get help converting your military experience into civilian language. Career Transition Partnership (CTP) provides this service — use it before you leave.

Fair Start Scotland is a free employment support programme. Veterans are eligible from the day of discharge — no waiting period.

The Scottish Government guarantees interviews for veterans who meet the minimum job criteria for public sector roles. Many Scottish councils and NHS boards follow the same commitment.

VALOUR — finding support locally

VALOUR is a new UK programme that coordinates veterans support through recognised local centres, regional field officers, and a national hub. The aim is a no-wrong-door approach — wherever you walk in, you can be connected to what you need.

Scotland's first VALOUR-recognised organisation (Round 1, announced March 2026) is SSAFA.

Find current recognised centres and field officers at gov.uk/guidance/valour-information-and-next-steps.

If your partner is also navigating this

▼

If your partner is also navigating this

The transition affects your partner too — and the support system was not designed with them in mind.

If your partner has been following the posting cycle — moving every few years, gaps in employment, interrupted NI contributions — they may have entitlements they don't know about.

NI record gaps: check your partner's NI record at gov.uk/check-national-insurance-record. Gaps from years of following postings may be fillable. This affects future pension entitlements.

Their own UC entitlement: if your partner is not working during the transition period, they may have their own UC eligibility independent of yours. Get advice on a joint versus separate claim.

Their own mental health support: V1P supports family members worried about a veteran. Your partner does not need to present as a veteran to access support — they need to be affected by the transition.
veteransfirstpoint.org.uk/get-support

Employment gaps: Forces Families Jobs (forcesfamiliesjobs.co.uk) specifically supports partners returning to work after service life. Employers on the site understand posting-cycle employment histories.

If you have children: service children have specific rights around school placement in Scotland. If your child is moving schools mid-year, ask the council about the service child priority provisions in school admissions.

If you find Op COURAGE in your search — it is England only

Op COURAGE is the NHS veterans mental health service for England. To use it you must be resident in England and registered with an English GP. In Scotland, your route is Veterans First Point and NHS Scotland. If you are moving from Scotland to England, confirm your GP registration and local NHS pathway before assuming Op COURAGE applies to you.

Veterans Mental Health and Wellbeing Pathway watch note

A new Veterans Mental Health and Wellbeing Pathway for Scotland is expected to launch in 2026, building on existing Veterans First Point provision. Until it launches, Veterans First Point remains your main mental health route.

Legal and justice — if you need it

This section is here because it is needed, not because it applies to everyone.

If you are in contact with the criminal justice system: Veteran status is relevant and you are entitled to raise it. The Armed Forces Covenant legal duty now covers criminal justice.

Legal aid in Scotland: Free legal representation for eligible cases. slab.org.uk

Debt and court orders: Citizens Advice Scotland and local money advice services can help before debt reaches court. The Accountant in Bankruptcy's Breathing Space scheme can pause creditor action. aib.gov.uk

Disclosure and employment: Not everything has to be disclosed forever. mygov.scot/find-out-about-disclosure

Veteran status does not mean special treatment — it means the system is supposed to understand your context.

Ask your GP practice to flag your veteran status on your record. This unlocks priority referral pathways for mental health and other veteran-specific services.

Find your local Veterans First Point centre at veteransfirstpoint.org.uk/get-support — self-referral, no GP required. V1P also supports family members worried about a veteran.

Get a review for long-term service injuries — VISIN

If a military-related injury or condition has not been fully resolved, ask to be referred to the Veterans In-Service Injury Network (VISIN). Hosted by NHS Highland with Scottish Government funding, VISIN provides an independent multidisciplinary clinical review. Ask your GP or health point of contact to refer you.

Women veterans

If you are a woman veteran, the standard transition checklist applies to you — but some things work differently in practice.

Tell your GP you are a veteran. GPs may not ask. Service-related mental health conditions in women are often misread as stress, anxiety, or relationship difficulties. Telling your GP explicitly, and asking for your veteran status to be flagged on your NHS Scotland record, means you are more likely to be routed to the right support.

Veterans First Point works with women veterans. V1P staff are trained in veteran-specific presentations. Self-referral — no GP required.

If you have caring responsibilities that accumulated during the posting cycle — including gaps in your NI record or employment history — these may affect your benefit entitlements. Get advice specific to your situation from Citizens Advice Scotland before claiming.

The Scottish Government has asked Veterans Scotland to improve inclusion of female veterans and recognition of different service experiences. If you feel a service is not recognising your experience, you are entitled to say so.

If you find Op COURAGE in your search — it is England only

Op COURAGE is the NHS veterans mental health service for England. To use it you must be resident in England and registered with an English GP. In Scotland, your route is Veterans First Point and NHS Scotland. If you are moving from Scotland to England, confirm your GP registration and local NHS pathway before assuming Op COURAGE applies to you.

If you need help tonight

If things feel urgent, these lines are free, available around the clock, and used to talking with veterans.

  • Veterans Gateway: 0808 802 1212 (24/7)
  • Samaritans: 116 123 (free, 24/7)
  • NHS 24: 111
  • Emergency: 999

VALOUR — finding support locally

VALOUR is a new UK programme that coordinates veterans support through recognised local centres, regional field officers, and a national hub. The aim is a no-wrong-door approach — wherever you walk in, you can be connected to what you need.

Common questions

▼
Do I qualify for council housing as a veteran? +
Yes — Scottish councils are required to give reasonable preference to veterans who are homeless or at risk of homelessness. The Veterans Homelessness Prevention Pathway means your discharge date should trigger a housing referral. Contact Housing Options Scotland Military Matters for free advice.
Is ADP the same as PIP? +
Adult Disability Payment (ADP) has replaced PIP in Scotland. The eligibility criteria are broadly similar but the process is handled by Social Security Scotland, not DWP. If you are already on PIP in Scotland, you will be transferred to ADP automatically. New applications should go to Social Security Scotland at mygov.scot/adult-disability-payment. Income and savings are not taken into account. You can be working or not working — it makes no difference to eligibility. ADP has two components: daily living and mobility. It is not a veteran-specific payment — it covers any disability or long-term health condition, including those related to service.
I'm struggling mentally. Where do I go first? +
Find your local Veterans First Point centre at veteransfirstpoint.org.uk/get-support — self-referral, no GP required. V1P also supports family members worried about a veteran. If you are in crisis, call Samaritans on 116 123 (free, 24/7), NHS 24 on 111, or 999 in an emergency. Veterans Gateway (0808 802 1212) can also connect you to the right service.
What is the Armed Forces Compensation Scheme? +
AFCS provides compensation for injuries, illness, and death caused by service on or after 6 April 2005. Claims must be made within 7 years of leaving service or the date you first knew your condition was linked to service. Contact Veterans UK on 0808 1914 218 (free).
I have debt. What should I do? +
Contact Credit Their Service (free specialist financial guidance for veterans), Citizens Advice Scotland, or Erskine Veterans Charity for welfare support. Do not ignore debt — it is much easier to deal with early. If you have debt concerns, contact Citizens Advice Scotland or a local money advice service early. The Accountant in Bankruptcy's Breathing Space scheme can pause creditor action while you get advice (aib.gov.uk).
